Limitations of this tracker

  • Days-open is a proxy, not a confirmed fill time. Adzuna's API returns when a posting was first indexed, not when it closes or fills. "Days open" here means "days since Adzuna first saw it," which will overstate true time-to-fill for postings that are relisted, and understate it for postings Adzuna indexed late.
  • Keyword matching, not semantic role classification. Each role bucket is a free-text search term (e.g. "release manager") matched against title and description. A senior title with an unconventional name may be missed; a junior role that happens to mention "PMO" in passing may be over-counted.
  • Coverage depends on what Adzuna indexes. Adzuna aggregates from job boards and employer sites but does not capture every posting — LinkedIn-only or recruiter-exclusive listings are frequently absent.
  • City assignment uses the posting's stated location area, not always the exact office; remote-first postings may cluster around a nominal HQ city.
  • Small cities and small role buckets are noisy. A city or role with under ~10 postings can shift a lot day to day and shouldn't be read as a trend.
  • Short history window. The trend chart keeps roughly the last 90 daily snapshots — meaningful once a few weeks have accumulated, but there's no data from before this feature was added, and it only tracks total volume, not a breakdown by role or city over time.
  • Salary figures are sparse. Many postings in this market omit salary information entirely, so any salary-based cut would be built on a small, self-selecting subset.

What this score actually measures

  • This is a simple weighted keyword-overlap heuristic — not a simulation of any specific company's real ATS software, which can use very different (and often undisclosed) matching logic.
  • It rewards exact and near-exact terminology. A genuine skill described in different words (e.g. "led cross-team rollouts" vs. "stakeholder management") may be flagged as a gap even if you actually have that experience — read the gap list with that in mind rather than copying every term verbatim.
  • Tool/certification acronyms (e.g. SAP, AWS, PMP) are weighted heavily even if mentioned only once in the job description, since those tend to be hard filters in real ATS systems.
